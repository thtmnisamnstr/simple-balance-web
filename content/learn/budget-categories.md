A budget category is a label for what your money was for, like groceries,
rent or gas. Every purchase gets one. At the end of the month you add up each
label, and you can see where your money went.

The hard part is picking the list. With too few labels, everything ends up in
"Shopping." With too many, you spend more time sorting than you save.

## Why it matters

Your bank statement is a list of store names in the order you visited them,
which doesn't tell you much. Categories turn it into an answer, like "we spent
$640 on food last month, and $260 of it was takeout."

They also let you compare one month with the next. If groceries are always
their own category, you can watch them go up in December and back down in
January. That only works if the names stay the same.

And categories are what a budget is built on. You can't set a limit for
eating out until eating out has a category of its own.

## Where most people's money goes

A few categories take up most of the money. In 2024 the average U.S. household
spent $78,535, according to the
[Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cesan.nr0.htm).
A third of that went to housing, about 17% to getting around, and about 13% to
food. More of the food money went to groceries than to eating out. Together,
those three made up more than 60% of all spending. Your own numbers will be
different, but those are the categories to set up first.

## A starter list

Most people need something close to this list. It's a lot like the one on the
Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's
[budget worksheet](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_well-being_monthly-budget.pdf).
You don't need every line, and you can rename anything.

**Home**

- Rent or mortgage
- Utilities: power, gas, water, trash
- Phone and internet
- Renters or homeowners insurance
- Repairs and supplies

**Food**

- Groceries
- Eating out, including takeout and coffee

**Getting around**

- Car payment
- Gas and charging
- Car insurance
- Car repairs and registration
- Bus, train and rideshare

**Health**

- Health insurance you pay for yourself
- Doctor, dentist and eye care
- Prescriptions

**Family and pets**

- Childcare
- Kids' activities and school costs
- Pet food and vet bills

**Personal**

- Clothes and shoes
- Haircuts and personal care
- Gifts and donations

**Fun**

- Streaming and other subscriptions
- Hobbies and going out
- Travel

**Everything else**

- Bank fees and interest
- Other

Keep the list for money coming in short: your paycheck, any side work, and
interest. A refund doesn't need an income category. It goes back into the
category you spent it from, as
[how to track your spending](/learn/track-your-spending/) explains.

## How to pick your categories

### 1. Start from your own statements

Look at a month or two of real spending before you choose. A list copied from
somewhere else will have categories you never use and miss some you need.

### 2. Start with about ten

You can always add more later. Ten labels are easy to keep up with. Thirty
make every receipt a decision.

### 3. Split a category when it's hiding something

"Food" works fine until you want to know how much of it is takeout. That's when
to split it into Groceries and Eating out. If you never wonder, you don't need
the split.

<Diagram name="budget-categories-split" />

### 4. Decide the tricky ones once

One trip to a big store might be food, cleaning supplies and a shirt. A phone
bill might be personal or for work. Choose a rule the first time and stick to
it, so the same thing lands in the same place every month.

### 5. Leave transfers out

Paying your credit card or moving money into savings isn't spending. If you
give it a spending category, you'll count the same money twice.

### 6. Keep an eye on "Other"

A little in Other is fine. If it becomes one of your biggest categories,
something in there deserves its own name.

### 7. Group them for the big picture

Ten categories are easier to read as three or four groups, like Home, Food and
Fun. You can then budget a whole group at once, or each category in it.

## How to do it in Simple Balance

You don't have to set up categories first. Type a new name in the category box
when you add a transaction, and Simple Balance creates it. Bring in a bank file
that has a category column, and those names come in too.

When you type a new category name on a deposit, the form asks what kind of
money it is. Choose **Money you earned** for pay. Choose **A refund of money
you spent** for a refund, so it lowers your spending instead of counting as
income.

### Set them up ahead of time

Open [**Categories**](/docs/categories-and-payees/). In the form for a new
category, type a **Category name** and choose what it **Applies to**:
**Expense**, **Income**, or **Income or expense**. Then press **Add category**.

### Make groups

On the same page, type a **Group name** and press **Add group**. You'll also
choose how the group is budgeted. **Has a budget of its own** means one number
for the whole group. **Adds up its categories' budgets** means you budget each
category inside it, and the group shows the total.

Then use the group picker on each category's row to put it in a group. Moving
a category changes the group's totals for past months too, so you can
rearrange your groups whenever you like.

### Clean up

If you end up with two categories for the same thing, like Dining and Eating
out, check the box next to each on **Categories**. Choose the one to keep and
press **Merge**. Every transaction moves to the one you kept.

To change the category on lots of purchases at once, go to
[**Transactions**](/docs/transactions/), check the rows, and change them
together. When one receipt covers two categories, press **Split across
categories** in the transaction form and give each part its own amount.

A category you've stopped using can be archived from its row. It leaves the
list, but its history stays. Turn on **Show archived** to see it again.

### See where the money went

[**Overview**](/docs/the-overview/) shows spending by category for any dates you
pick. The **Categories** report under [**Reports**](/docs/reports/) goes into
more detail. Transfers between your own accounts don't show up in either,
because they don't have a category.
