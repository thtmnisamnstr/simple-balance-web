The 50/30/20 rule is the shortest budget there is: three numbers instead of
thirty. Split what you take home into needs, wants and savings, and keep each
near its share.

## What it is

The rule comes from _All Your Worth_, a 2005 book by Elizabeth Warren and her
daughter Amelia Warren Tyagi, where it's called the balanced money formula. It
divides your **after-tax** income, the amount that actually reaches your
account, three ways:

- **50% for needs.** The things you'd still have to pay if you lost your job
  next month: rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, getting to
  work, childcare, and the **minimum** payment on every debt.
- **30% for wants.** Everything you could stop paying for if you had to:
  eating out, streaming, travel, hobbies, the nicer version of something you
  need.
- **20% for savings and debt.** Building an emergency fund, saving for
  retirement, and any payment on a debt **above** the minimum.

So on $4,000 a month take-home pay, that's $2,000 for needs, $1,200 for wants
and $800 for savings and extra debt payments.

## Why it matters

**It's a budget you can keep in your head.** Most people stop budgeting
because there are too many numbers to look after. Three is a number anyone can
check.

**Wants get a real share.** A plan with no room for anything enjoyable gets
abandoned. Giving wants 30% on purpose means a dinner out isn't a failure.

**Saving comes first, not last.** Saving a fixed share every month is how an
emergency fund gets built, rather than hoping something is left over.

**It tells you what's out of balance.** If needs take 65%, that isn't a
spending problem you can fix by skipping coffee. It's rent, or a car payment,
and it tells you where the big decision is.

## How to do it

### 1. Work out your monthly take-home pay

Add up what landed in your account last month from work. If you're paid every
two weeks, multiply one paycheck by 26 and divide by 12, rather than counting
two paychecks a month.

### 2. Multiply

Half, three tenths, one fifth. Write the three targets down.

### 3. Sort last month's spending into the three

Go through a month of spending and label each category need, want or saving.
Some things are both: groceries are a need, but the expensive version of
groceries is partly a want. Pick one and be consistent. The rule is a guide,
not an audit.

### 4. Compare, and adjust

Most people's first comparison shows needs above 50%. That's normal in an
expensive city. You can bend the split to 60/20/20, as long as you decide it
rather than drift into it. The one number worth protecting is the 20%.

### 5. Move the 20% on payday

Set up an automatic transfer to savings the day you're paid. Money that's
already gone can't be spent by accident.

<Callout kind="note" title="Debt payments go in two places">
The minimum payment on a credit card or loan is a need, because you have to
pay it. Anything you pay above the minimum counts toward the 20%, because it
does the same job as saving: it leaves you better off next month.
</Callout>

## How to do it in Simple Balance

Simple Balance doesn't have a 50/30/20 button. It has the two pieces the rule
is made of: groups of categories, and budgets that can be a share of your
income.

**Make the groups.** Open **Categories** and press **Add group** to make a
group called Needs, then another called Wants. When it asks how the group is
budgeted, choose **Has a budget of its own**: one number for the whole group.
Then use the group picker on each category's row to put it in Needs or Wants.

**Budget each group as a share of income.** On **Budgets**, under **Set a
budget**, choose the Needs group. Under **Amount decided by**, choose **A share
of the income before it** and enter 50. Do the same for Wants with 30. The
budget reads last month's income rather than this month's, because a share of
a month that's still running would change every time you looked at it.

**Read the 20% off your reports.** Moving money into savings is a transfer
between two of your own accounts, so it isn't spending, and there's nothing for
a Savings budget to count. The 20% shows up instead as the gap between what
came in and what went out. **Reports** has **Income and expenses**: group it by
month, and the difference each month should be at least a fifth of the income.

**Record extra debt payments as transfers too.** Paying a loan or a card from
checking is money moving between two of your accounts, so it's a **Transfer**.
The payment shows on the loan's balance going down, and on your
[net worth](/learn/net-worth/) going up.

**Check in on the groups.** The **Budgets** page shows each group's line above
its categories, with what the categories spent between them. A group with a
budget of its own is badged **Own budget**.
