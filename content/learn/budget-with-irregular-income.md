A lot of budgeting advice assumes the same paycheck lands on the same day every
month. If you freelance, work for tips or commission, run a small business, or
get paid every other week, your pay doesn't work that way. You can still
budget. You just plan up from your worst month instead of down from your
average one.

## Why it matters

Budgeting from your average month leaves you short in the slow months. Say you
earned $3,000 one month and $5,000 the next. Planning to spend $4,000 works in
the good month and falls short in the bad one. The bad month is the one where
spending ends up on a credit card.

<Diagram name="budget-with-irregular-income-floor" />

Good months can disappear, too. Without a plan, a big month feels like a raise
and gets spent like one. Then a slow month comes and there's nothing set
aside.

Taxes can be a surprise. If nobody takes tax out of what you're paid, as with
most freelance work, part of every payment isn't yours to spend.

## How to do it

### 1. Find your floor

Look at the last six months and find the month you earned the least. That's
your floor, the amount you can count on.
[Penn State Extension](https://extension.psu.edu/budgeting-with-irregular-income)
suggests using exactly that number as your expected income.

### 2. Make a bare-bones budget that fits under it

List rent, utilities, groceries, transportation, insurance and minimum debt
payments. These essentials should fit inside your lowest month. If they don't,
work on that first, because no budgeting method can fix it.

### 3. Rank everything else

Write down what gets money next, in order. For example: a cushion of savings,
then extra debt payments, then the fun stuff. In a good month you get further
down the list. In a slow month you stop sooner, and you already know where.

### 4. Set aside money for taxes first

If you work for yourself, there's no employer
[taking tax out for you](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center).
If you expect to owe $1,000 or more when you file, the IRS generally wants you
to pay [estimated tax](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes)
during the year, in four payments. Put part of every payment into a separate
account before you budget the rest. How much depends on where you live and how
much you earn, so it's worth asking a tax preparer once.

### 5. Build a cushion, then budget from last month

[Penn State Extension](https://extension.psu.edu/budgeting-with-irregular-income)
suggests an [emergency fund](/learn/emergency-fund/) of at least one to three
months of your average income. It also suggests keeping the extra from bigger
paychecks as a cushion for the smaller ones. Once your cushion covers a month of
spending, you can budget each month from what you earned the month before. You
always know the amount before the month starts, because the money is already in
the bank. Getting there usually takes a few months of saving extra from the good
ones.

### 6. If you're paid every two weeks

Every two weeks works out to 26 paychecks a year. So in most years,
[two months have three paydays](https://financeadmin.lehigh.edu/content/semi-monthly-bi-weekly-faq)
instead of two. Budget each month on two paychecks, and treat the third as
extra. Put it toward savings, a debt, or your cushion.

## How to do it in Simple Balance

### Record income as it comes in

Each payment is a **Deposit** with an income category, like Freelance or Tips.
If you're paid on a schedule, set it up once on
[**Recurring**](/docs/recurring-transactions/). It can repeat every two weeks as
well as every month. If the amount changes, leave it blank. On each payday, a
row shows up in [**Staged**](/docs/staged-transactions/) for you to fill in with
the real amount.

### Budget from last month's income

On [**Budgets**](/docs/budgets/), under **Amount decided by**, choose **A share
of the income before it**. Then a rule like "groceries get 15% of what came in
last month" sets the budget for you each month. It uses last month because a
share of a month that's still going would keep changing.

### Decide what gets paid first

Give your most important budgets a **Funded first** number. Lower numbers go
first. The **Budgets** page then shows how much of each budget the month's
income covers, filling them in your order until the money runs out. Put rent
and groceries first, and in a slow month you'll see exactly which budgets
didn't get covered.

### Let a budget follow your history

Choose **What the last few periods spent** under **Amount decided by**, and
the budget becomes the average of your last few months. That suits spending
that rises and falls with your income.

### Look ahead

The **What happens next** panel on **Budgets** projects your balances month by
month. It starts from what your accounts hold today and adds your recurring
transactions and what you usually earn and spend. None of it has happened yet.
It's a picture of where things are headed if nothing changes.

[Saving goals, automatic amounts and the forecast](/docs/planning-ahead/) in the
docs explains each of these in more detail.
