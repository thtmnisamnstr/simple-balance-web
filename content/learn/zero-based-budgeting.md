In a zero-based budget, you decide what every dollar you have is for: rent,
groceries, the emergency fund, next summer's trip. You keep going until there's
no money left without a plan.

## What it is

The idea started in business. Peter Pyhrr wrote about it in a 1970
[_Harvard Business Review_ article](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/zero-base-budgeting-pyhrr-peter/10002660711)
called "Zero-Base Budgeting." Instead of starting from last year's budget,
every cost starts at zero and has to earn its place.

At home it's simpler. Suzanne Holloway, an extension agent at the University of
Florida, [describes it](https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/leonco/2025/09/12/budgeting-basics/) as a budget that "assigns every dollar earned a
purpose." You can think of each purpose as a job for that money. In numbers,
it looks like this:

**Money coming in, minus every dollar you've assigned, equals zero.**

The zero is the money without a plan. Your bank balance doesn't go to zero.
Say you have $3,000 in checking. You might assign $1,400 to rent, $500 to
groceries, $300 to the car, $200 to fun and $600 to savings. Nothing is left
unassigned, but the money is all still there. It's just spoken for.

## Why it matters

Money without a job tends to get spent on whatever comes up. Giving it a job
before the month starts keeps that from happening.

Savings gets a job like everything else. In a zero-based budget, the
[emergency fund](/learn/emergency-fund/) isn't whatever's left over at the end.
It's a line you put money into, just like rent.

When you overspend, the money has to come from somewhere. If groceries run $60
over, you take $60 from another job. That makes it a choice you make on
purpose, not a surprise at the end of the month.

<Diagram name="zero-based-budgeting-move" />

It also works when your pay changes. You only assign money you already have,
so a small month just means fewer jobs get money.

## How to do it

### 1. Start with the money you have

Use what's in your accounts now, or this month's pay once it arrives. Only
budget money you already have, not money you're expecting.

### 2. List the jobs

Put the fixed bills first, then everyday needs like groceries and gas, then
savings goals and extra debt payments, then the fun stuff. Bills that don't
come every month, like insurance or holiday gifts, get a little money each
month too.

### 3. Assign money until nothing is left

Go down the list and give each job an amount. If you run out of money before
you reach the end, the last items wait until next time. If money is left
after the last item, give it a job too, like extra savings or paying down a
debt.

### 4. Move money when plans change

When a category runs over, take the difference from another one and write
down that you did. Most of zero-based budgeting is this one habit.

### 5. Give new money a job right away

A refund, a gift or money from a side job gets a job the day it arrives.

## How to do it in Simple Balance

To use Simple Balance as a zero-based budgeting app, two features work
together. One is budgets that
carry over from month to month. The other is a number called **left to
assign**.

### Turn on carrying

On [**Budgets**](/docs/budgets/), when you set or edit a budget, check
**Carry what is left over into the next month**. That turns the budget into an
envelope. Whatever you don't spend this month is added to next month, and
anything you overspend is taken off next month.

### Watch what's left to assign

Once any budget carries over, the **Budgets** page shows how much is **left to
assign**. That's the money in the accounts your budget covers, minus what your
envelopes already hold. Your goal is to bring it to zero by giving the rest a
job.

### Choose which accounts count

Each account's form has a box called **The budget is about the money in this
account**. Leave it on for checking, savings you spend from, and credit cards.
Card spending empties an envelope right away, even though the cash leaves
later when you pay the bill. Turn the box off for money that isn't part of
your plan, like a retirement account.

Your accounts are on the [**Accounts**](/docs/accounts/) page, grouped by
type. The box is in each account's form.

### Move money between jobs

When one category runs over and you want to cover it from another, change both
amounts for this month only. Press the calendar button at the end of each
budget's row and type the new amount. Next month, both go back to normal.

### Set a limit on an envelope

**Most to carry** stops an envelope from growing forever. It works both ways.
It caps money that piles up in a fund you never use, and it caps overspending
that never gets paid back.

[What is envelope budgeting?](/learn/envelope-budgeting/) looks at the same
idea from another angle. The [Budgets](/docs/budgets/) page in the
documentation explains exactly how left to assign is worked out.
