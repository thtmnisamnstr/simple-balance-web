Subscriptions are designed to be easy to start and easy to forget. Each one is
small, most renew without asking, and a free trial becomes a charge on a date
nobody wrote down. Finding them all takes an hour. Keeping the list short takes
a habit.

## Why it matters

**People underestimate them.** In a 2022 survey by C+R Research, people's
first guess at what they spent on subscriptions each month averaged $86. Asked
again, category by category, the average came to $219. Both numbers are what
people said rather than what their statements showed, which is the point: the
real figure is the one you find by looking.

**They add up over a year.** A $15 subscription you don't use is $180 a year.
Three of them pay for a weekend away.

**Prices go up quietly.** A subscription that started at one price often
renews at a higher one, with an email you may not have read.

**Annual renewals are the biggest surprise.** A yearly charge doesn't show up
in a month of statements, so it's the easiest to miss and usually the largest.

## How to do it

### 1. Pull a year of statements

Two or three months finds the monthly charges. A full year finds the annual
ones. Include every checking account and every card, plus any online payment
account you use.

### 2. Look for repeats

Search for the same name or the same amount turning up month after month.
Look for the words subscription, membership, renewal and premium, and for
charges from app stores, which bill many subscriptions under one name. Your
phone's app store account lists the subscriptions it bills for you.

### 3. Make a list

For each one: what it is, how much, how often, when it renews, and which card
it's on. The list is the part most people skip.

### 4. Decide, one at a time

Keep it, downgrade it, or cancel it. If you haven't used it in a month and
wouldn't notice it gone, that's an answer. Cancel through the company's own
website or app, and keep the confirmation.

### 5. Keep the list from growing back

Before starting a free trial, write down the date it becomes a charge and set
a reminder a few days before. Then go through the list every few months.

## How to do it in Simple Balance

Simple Balance doesn't find subscriptions on its own and can't cancel
anything. What it gives you is a place to see every payment in one list, and a
way to keep the ones you keep in front of you.

**Start from your payees.** **Payees** lists everyone you've paid, with how
many transactions each has. Open one to see every payment to them, which is
how a monthly charge you'd forgotten shows itself.

**Search for the rest.** On **Transactions**, search by payee, description or
notes, and sort by any column. Sorting by amount puts charges of the same size
next to each other, which is a quick way to find one that repeats under
slightly different names.

**Give them one category.** Make a Subscriptions category. On
**Transactions**, check the rows that belong in it and recategorize them all
at once. The **Categories** report on **Reports** then shows what they cost
together.

**Put the keepers on Recurring.** For each subscription you keep, open the
menu on one of its transactions and choose **Save as recurring transaction**.
The **Recurring** page becomes your subscription list. Each one proposes a row
on **Staged** when it's due, waiting for you to approve it, and can email you
when it does.

**Get reminded before a trial ends.** A **template** can carry an email
reminder for a single date. Save one for the trial, set the reminder a few
days before the charge, and you'll hear about it while you can still cancel.
