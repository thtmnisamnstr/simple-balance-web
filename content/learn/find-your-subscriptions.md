Subscriptions are easy to start and easy to forget. Each one is small, most
renew on their own, and a free trial turns into a charge on a date nobody wrote
down. To find them, go through a year of bank and card statements and list every
charge that repeats. That takes about an hour. Keeping the list short takes a
habit.

## Why it matters

People tend to guess low. In a [2022 survey by C+R Research](https://www.crresearch.com/blog/subscription-service-statistics-and-costs/), people's
first guess at what they spent on subscriptions each month averaged $86. When
they went through them one kind at a time, the average came to $219. That's
[$133 more](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/09/06/consumers-underestimate-monthly-subscription-costs-by-at-least-100.html) than they first thought. Both numbers came from what people
remembered, not from their statements, so the only way to know your real
number is to look.

<Diagram name="find-your-subscriptions-guess" />

They add up over a year. A $15 subscription you don't use costs $180 a year.
Three of those could pay for a weekend away.

Prices can creep up. A subscription can renew at a higher price than the one
you signed up for, with an email about it that you may never have opened.

Yearly renewals are the easiest to miss. A charge that comes once a year won't
show up in a month or two of statements, and when it does come, it's all at
once.

## How to do it

### 1. Pull a year of statements

Two or three months will show the monthly charges. A full year shows the
yearly ones too. Include every checking account and every card, plus any
online payment account you use.

<Diagram name="find-your-subscriptions-statements" />

### 2. Look for repeats

Look for the same name or the same amount coming up month after month. Watch
for words like subscription, membership, renewal and premium. Also look for
app store charges, which can bundle many subscriptions under one name. Your
phone's app store account lists the subscriptions it bills you for.

### 3. Make a list

For each subscription, write down what it is, how much it costs, how often you
pay, when it renews, and which card it's on.

### 4. Decide on each one

Keep it, switch to a cheaper plan, or cancel it. If you haven't used it in a
month and wouldn't miss it, that's your answer. Cancel through the company's
own website or app, and save the confirmation.

### 5. Keep the list from growing back

Before you start a free trial, write down the date it turns into a charge and
set a reminder for a few days before. The Federal Trade Commission's advice on
[free trials and auto-renewals](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/getting-and-out-free-trials-auto-renewals-and-negative-option-subscriptions) is blunt: if you don't cancel on time,
you'll probably be charged. It also suggests watching your card statements
after you cancel, in case the charges keep coming. Then look over your list
every few months.

## How to do it in Simple Balance

Simple Balance isn't a subscription tracker that finds them for you, and it
can't cancel anything.
What it does give you is one place to see every payment, and a way to keep the
subscriptions you keep in plain view.

### Start with your payees

[**Payees**](/docs/categories-and-payees/#payees) lists everyone you've paid,
with how many transactions each one has. Open one to see every payment you've
made to them. That's often how a monthly charge you forgot about turns up.

### Search for the rest

On [**Transactions**](/docs/transactions/#find-a-transaction), you can search by
payee, description or notes, and sort by any column. Sorting by amount puts
charges of the same size next to each other. That's a quick way to spot one that
repeats under slightly different names.

### Put them in one category

Make a category called Subscriptions. On **Transactions**, check the rows that
belong in it and
[change their category all at once](/docs/changing-many-transactions/#example-recategorize-everything-from-one-store).
The **Categories** report under [**Reports**](/docs/reports/) then shows what
they cost together.

### Track the ones you keep

For each subscription you keep, open the menu on one of its transactions and
choose **Save as recurring transaction**. Your
[**Recurring**](/docs/recurring-transactions/) page becomes your subscription
list. Each one adds a row to [**Staged**](/docs/staged-transactions/) when it's
due, for you to check and approve, and it can send you an email when it does.

### Get a reminder before a trial ends

A [**template**](/docs/templates/#email-reminders) can send you an email
reminder on a single date. Make one for the trial, set the reminder for a few
days before the first charge, and you'll hear about it while you can still
cancel.
