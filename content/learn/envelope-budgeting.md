Envelope budgeting is the oldest budget there is. On payday you take out cash,
divide it into envelopes labeled Groceries, Gas and Fun, and spend from each
envelope until it's empty. When it's empty, you stop. Lately it's had a second
life online as "cash stuffing."

## What it is

The method has two rules, and everything else is detail:

1. **Each envelope holds the money for one kind of spending.** You spend
   groceries money from the groceries envelope and nowhere else.
2. **What's left at the end of the month stays in the envelope.** It doesn't
   go back into a general pile. If you spent $340 of a $400 grocery envelope,
   next month starts with $60 already in it.

The second rule is the one most budgets skip, and it's what makes envelopes
work.

## Why it matters

**The limit is real.** A number on a spreadsheet is easy to ignore. An empty
envelope isn't. Even without the cash, keeping the rule that an empty envelope
means stop is what makes the budget mean something.

**Saving a little in a quiet month counts.** Because leftovers carry forward,
spending less on gas in March gives you more for gas in April. In a budget that
resets every month, the saving just disappears.

**Overspending is visible.** If you borrow from the Fun envelope to cover
groceries, you know exactly what it cost you.

**It handles irregular costs.** An envelope for car repairs that gets $50 a
month and isn't touched for a year has $600 in it when the brakes go.

## How to do it

### 1. Choose which spending gets an envelope

Envelopes work best for spending that varies and that you control: groceries,
eating out, gas, household supplies, personal spending. Bills that are paid
automatically, like rent or insurance, can stay in your checking account.

### 2. Decide how much goes in each

Use what you've actually spent over the last few months.
[How to track your spending](/learn/track-your-spending/) is how you find out.

### 3. Fill the envelopes on payday

With cash, that's a trip to the bank. Without cash, it's writing down how much
of your checking balance belongs to each envelope.

### 4. Spend only from the right envelope

And when one's empty, stop, or move money from another envelope on purpose
and write down that you did.

### 5. Carry the leftovers

Don't sweep the remainder back. Leave it in the envelope, or move it to an
envelope for something you're saving toward.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Cash has costs too">
Cash can be lost or stolen and earns nothing sitting in an envelope. Paying
bills online with it is awkward. Many people keep envelopes for a few
day-to-day categories and use their account for everything else, or keep the
envelopes as numbers rather than bills.
</Callout>

## How to do it in Simple Balance

In Simple Balance, an envelope is a budget that carries.

**Make a budget carry.** On **Budgets**, set a budget on a category and check
**Carry what is left over into the next month**. The $50 you didn't spend on
groceries in March is $50 more grocery money in April. Overspending carries
too, as money the next month owes, rather than being forgotten when the
calendar turns over.

**Cap an envelope if you want.** **Most to carry** limits how much an
envelope can hold, in both directions, so an envelope nobody touches doesn't
grow forever and an overspent one doesn't sink forever.

**See what's left to fill envelopes with.** Once a budget carries, the
**Budgets** page shows **left to assign**: the money in the accounts your
budget covers, minus what the envelopes already hold. That's
[zero-based budgeting](/learn/zero-based-budgeting/), and it's the digital
version of the cash you haven't put in an envelope yet.

**Keep real cash on the books.** If you do use cash, add an account of type
**Cash** for your wallet or your envelopes. Taking money out at an ATM is a
**Transfer** from checking to Cash, because it's still your money. Spending it
is a **Withdrawal** from Cash with a category, which is what counts against the
envelope.

**Make cash quick to record.** Cash never shows up in a bank file, so it's the
spending most likely to go missing. Save a **template** for the cash purchases
you make often: open the menu on any transaction and choose **Save as
template**. Next time, pick it in the transaction form and it fills itself in.
Leave the amount blank and it asks you each time.
